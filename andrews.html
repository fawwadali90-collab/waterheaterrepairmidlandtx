Boom-Town Demand on Everyday Tanks

An Andrews water heater does not live an average life. During boom periods, rental houses run at full occupancy with workers on rotating shifts, which means back-to-back showers at odd hours and laundry running constantly. A standard tank sized for a family of four gets hammered by six adults on staggered schedules, and the recovery burner barely gets a rest.

We size for the load that actually exists. That often means stepping up to a 50-gallon high-recovery unit, and for the heaviest households it means tankless. A tankless system does not care how many showers happen in a row, which makes it a natural fit for workforce housing. We will ask who lives there and how they use hot water, then recommend accordingly.

Shift schedules create a pattern we see often in Andrews: the tank gets hammered at 6 a.m. and 6 p.m., then sits idle all day. That cycling is actually harder on a tank than steady use, because every recovery cycle is a full thermal swing that expands and contracts the steel. A tankless unit sidesteps the problem entirely, and a high-recovery tank at least shortens the strain. Either way, matching the equipment to the schedule extends its life.

Sand, Dust, and Burner Assemblies

Andrews County's sandy soil does not stay on the ground. Wind carries fine grit into garage installs, exterior enclosures, and vent terminations, where it settles on burner assemblies and clogs combustion air openings. A gas water heater starved of clean combustion air burns dirty, soots up, and can trip safety shutoffs or produce carbon monoxide concerns.

Part of every gas service we do in Andrews is a burner and vent inspection: cleaning the assembly, checking the vent draft, and making sure the termination is clear. On replacements we pay attention to vent placement relative to prevailing winds and drifting sand. It is unglamorous work that keeps the unit burning clean and safe.

Vent terminations deserve seasonal attention in Andrews County. After a dust storm, it is worth a glance to make sure the termination is not packed with sand, and we check it on every service visit regardless. A blocked termination can cause the unit to shut down or, worse, spill combustion gases. Thirty seconds with a flashlight a few times a year is cheap insurance.

Dust storms also punish outdoor tankless installs, where the intake filter can load up fast. We check and clean intake screens on every tankless service visit in Andrews County, and we show homeowners how to rinse the screen themselves between visits. It takes two minutes and it keeps the unit breathing easy through dust season.

Rental Turnover Replacements

Between tenants is the smartest time to replace a tired water heater in Andrews. The unit is accessible, the house is empty, and there is no tenant without hot water while the work happens. We coordinate with landlords and property managers on timing, and we can assess the unit during a turnover walkthrough before it becomes a mid-lease emergency.

What we look for during a turnover assessment is simple: the tank's age, signs of rust or weeping at seams and fittings, the condition of the anode rod, and whether the capacity matches the rental's typical occupancy. A tank past ten years in Andrews water with visible corrosion is a replacement candidate, not a gamble worth taking with a new lease starting. We quote it plainly and schedule it around your turnover window.

For landlords doing turnovers on a tight schedule, we offer a water heater condition report as a standalone visit. We come out between tenants, assess every unit in the property, and hand you a written summary: what is fine, what needs maintenance, and what should be replaced before the next lease. It costs far less than one emergency call and it lets you plan the work instead of reacting to it.

Hard Water, Short Anode Life

Andrews water carries the Permian Basin mineral signature, and it eats anode rods. The rod is the only thing standing between the water and the steel tank, and in this water a standard rod can be spent in two to three years. Once it is gone, the tank rusts from the inside, and the first symptom is often rusty hot water or a leak at a seam.

We check anodes on every maintenance visit and replace them before they are gone. It is a small part with an outsized job, and in Andrews it deserves more attention than the manual's generic schedule suggests. Combined with annual flushing, anode replacement is the cheapest life insurance a tank can get.

Power vent and direct vent units come up in Andrews when the ideal vent path is not available, such as a unit installed in an interior room far from an outside wall. These units use a fan to push exhaust out through a sidewall, which opens up placement options that atmospheric-vent units cannot use. We install and service power vent models and we will tell you when one is the right answer for a tricky layout.

Water testing is worth doing once if you have never had it done. A basic hardness and mineral panel costs little and it tells us exactly what your tank faces, which lets us set the right anode and flushing schedule instead of guessing from regional averages.

Our rental in Andrews keeps running out of hot water. What should we do?

The tank is almost certainly undersized for the occupancy. A 50-gallon high-recovery unit or a tankless system sized for the household usually solves it. We will assess the current setup and quote the right-sized options. A common Andrews setup that works well is a 50-gallon high-recovery gas unit for a three-bedroom rental, or a mid-size tankless for a house with four or more occupants on rotating shifts. Staggering shower times helps in the short term, but tenants will not do it reliably, so the durable fix is capacity.

Do you work with landlords in Andrews?

Yes, regularly. We handle emergency no-hot-water calls, planned replacements between tenants, and maintenance across rental portfolios, with written documentation for your records. We keep it simple for out-of-town owners too: photos of the old and new unit, a written summary of the work, and invoicing that matches the quote.

Why does my gas water heater keep shutting off?

In Andrews the common culprits are a dirty burner assembly clogged with windblown sand, a failing thermocouple, or a venting problem. We clean and inspect the burner, test the safety controls, and check vent draft during the visit. If the shutdowns started after a dust storm, sand in the burner assembly is the prime suspect; if they are random and year-round, the thermocouple or gas control valve moves to the top of the list.

How often should the anode rod be checked here?

Every two years at most in Andrews water, and yearly is not overkill. The minerals deplete rods fast, and catching a spent rod early is what keeps the tank from rusting through. If the rod is already gone and the tank is past ten years, replacement usually beats another rod, since the tank has been unprotected for a while. Homes with water softeners can check a little less often, since softened water is gentler on the rod.

Can you install a tankless unit in an older Andrews home?

Usually yes, pending gas line and venting verification. Tankless burners need more gas at peak draw than a tank burner, so we check line sizing first. We handle the full conversion including venting. The other check is electrical: tankless units need a standard outlet for their controls and freeze protection, which most homes have, but we verify it during the assessment. Either way, you will know the full scope and cost before we schedule the conversion, with no surprises on install day.
